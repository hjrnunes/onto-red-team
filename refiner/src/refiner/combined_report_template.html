Pipeline diagnostics and quality metrics for this run.

This report summarizes a red-team prompt generation pipeline run. The pipeline:

  1. Ingests a content policy document (what the AI system should and should not do)
  2. Matches each policy concept to known AI safety risks from standards frameworks (NIST AI RMF, OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, etc.)
  3. Anchors each risk to ontology classes (formal knowledge representations) and enumerates specific domain values
  4. Contextualizes the risk by assigning semantic roles (agent, object, instrument, etc.) and relevance scores
  5. Generates adversarial test prompts using LLM, grounded in the domain context
  6. Evaluates prompt quality via automated metrics and LLM-as-judge scoring

The goal is to produce realistic, diverse test prompts that probe the AI system's content policy boundaries without obvious red flags.

Risks matched from the knowledge graph for each policy concept, with match quality and cross-framework links.

The risk landscape maps each policy concept to known AI safety risks from 10+ standards frameworks (IBM Risk Atlas, NIST AI RMF, OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, etc.) using semantic search with LLM re-ranking.

  • Risk Registry — all matched risks with descriptions, cross-mappings, and related mitigation actions
  • Policy Mappings — which risks were matched to each policy concept, with relevance level and semantic distance
  • Distance — semantic search distance (0-1). <0.3 = strong match, 0.3-0.5 = moderate, >0.5 = weak
  • Weak Matches — risks with distance >0.4 that may not genuinely apply

Risk taxonomy with ontological domain context — cross-framework mappings and variation axes for prompt generation.

This tab combines the risk taxonomy with the domain context that drives prompt generation:

  • Entries — individual risks from AI safety standards, organized into groups
  • Cross-mappings — ground-truth links to equivalent risks in other frameworks (from the knowledge graph, not LLM): exact, broad, narrow, related
  • Variation axes — ontology-anchored dimensions of prompt diversity, each with a BFO category and semantic roles (agent, instrument, object)
  • Enumerations — concrete values for each axis. High-relevance values are sampled 3x more often during prompt generation
  • Origin — where each enumeration came from: an ontology with discovery method, or "LLM-generated"
entries

The enriched policy document extracted from the input, with structured concepts and boundary examples.

The ingest stage parses a content policy document and extracts structured information:

  • Context — organization, domain, purpose, AI systems, and regulations
  • Stakeholders — who governs, uses, and is affected by the AI system
  • Policy concepts — individual rules with definitions, boundary examples, and controls
  • Confidence labels — color-coded tags on each policy header: green = high confidence, amber = partial, red = not found

Boundary examples are particularly important — they calibrate the red-team prompt generator to produce prompts that land near the policy boundary.
